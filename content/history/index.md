# The history of the SS Georgette

On 1 December 1876 an iron screw-steamer built four years earlier on the Clyde was
run onto a beach on the south-west coast of Western Australia because there was
nothing else left to do with her. People drowned in the surf. A captain lost his
certificate. A sixteen-year-old girl became a legend, and the Aboriginal stockman
who raised the alarm did not.

A hundred and fifty years later the wreck is still there, a few metres under
Calgardup Bay, visible from the beach on perhaps a handful of days a year.

These pages set out what is known, what is disputed, and what the record does not
say.
