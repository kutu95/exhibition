# Calgardup Bay, Redgate Beach and Isaac Rock

The wreck site is not marked, not fenced, and not obvious. It is a surf beach that
several hundred thousand people visit without knowing what is under the water in
front of them. This page describes the place.

## Calgardup Bay

Calgardup Bay is the stretch of shoreline south of Margaret River, on the section
of coast now followed by the Cape to Cape Track. Redgate Beach is its best-known
access point; there is an interpretive plaque above the Redgate car park that gives
the wreck story in a compressed paragraph, in the version with Grace Bussell on the
horse.

It is granite-and-limestone country with reef close inshore, exposed to the full
Southern Ocean swell. That combination is why the Georgette's grounding here on
1 December 1876 killed people even after the ship reached the beach: the surf broke
the lifeboat against the hull before it was ever clear of the ship.

## Where the wreck lies

The Georgette lies a few metres beneath the surface, just offshore.

Whether she is visible depends on a coincidence of four conditions, none of which
the visitor controls:

- swell under about a metre
- clear water, with no sand suspended in it
- low tide
- the sand banks moved off the hull

Add an offshore breeze to flatten the surface, and the hull shows as a dark,
unmistakable line — from the beach on the best days, and clearly from the air. By
the author's own count that alignment occurs only a handful of times a year. It was
one such morning in February 2024, with a drone up, that began this whole project:
see [the author's preface](/book).

The photograph [Hiding in Plain Sight](/shop/redgate-beach-panorama-1-1) is made
directly over the site.

## Isaac Rock

Isaac Rock stands off the Calgardup Bay shoreline within sight of where the ship
came ashore.

At the time of the wreck it was known as **Black Rock**. Today it is called
**Isaac Rock** — one of very few features on this coast carrying the name of
[Sam Isaacs](/history/sam-isaacs), the Aboriginal stockman who raised the alarm and
went into the surf.

## The propeller under the sand

One of the Georgette's propellers is still somewhere in the sand at Redgate.

Divers attempting to salvage the pair in 1964 broke a blade off one of them in the
struggle; that propeller was not recovered, and the tides took it back. An attempt
to dig it up in the 1990s failed. Its position is not known.

## Visiting

The wreck site is offshore from Redgate Beach and is not accessible without
appropriate conditions and experience. There is nothing to see from the car park.

[EXPAND] If the wreck is a protected historic site under Commonwealth or WA

heritage and diving organisations.
