# The Court of Inquiry, December 1876

Within weeks of the wreck, a marine inquiry into the loss of the SS Georgette sat
in the small Busselton courtroom.

This page is potentially the most valuable on the whole site. Inquiry proceedings
are primary records, they are not well represented online for this wreck, and the
project has already worked through them — the [Captain Godfrey
installation](/installations/captain-godfrey) is built from them, and Marcia van
Zeller's research took her to the State Records Office of Western Australia, the
Battye Library, and the courtroom itself.

None of that is currently readable by a search engine. It is inside a gallery
installation and a novel.

## What the inquiry examined

[EXPAND — this is the core of the page and needs the author's material]

From what the site already states, the inquiry turned on the sequence aboard the
ship between the evening of 30 November and dawn on 1 December 1876:

- the leak reported by the Chief Engineer off Cape Naturaliste
- [Captain Godfrey's](/history/captain-john-godfrey) decision to order the pumps
  checked without going below himself
- the failure of the pumps after midnight
- the decision to beach the ship once the boiler fires were out
- the launching of the lifeboat and the gig, and the loss of life when the lifeboat
  stove in against the hull and capsized

## The finding

Godfrey was found guilty. His certificate was suspended. He never accepted the
verdict.

## What a transcript would add

If any part of the transcript can be reproduced or closely paraphrased, this page
should carry it. Specifically:

- **Who gave evidence.** A list of witnesses is itself a useful historical record
  and is exactly the kind of page other researchers link to.
- **The Chief Engineer's evidence**, which is the origin of the whole sequence.
- **Whether Sam Isaacs or Grace Bussell were called.** If the rescuers gave no
  evidence to the inquiry, that absence is significant, and saying so — with the
  record to back it — is a genuine contribution rather than a retelling.
- **George Leake's status.** He was a passenger who nearly drowned and later left a
  written account that [contradicted the press](/history/grace-bussell). Whether the
  court heard from him matters.

## Where the records are
