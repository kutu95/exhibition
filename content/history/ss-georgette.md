# SS Georgette

An iron screw-steamer, built on the Clyde in 1872 and lost on the south-west coast
of Western Australia four years later. This page collects what is known about the
ship herself, as distinct from [the wreck and the rescue](/story).

## Built at Dumbarton, 1872

The Georgette was built in 1872 at Dumbarton, on the south bank of the Clyde, at a
new yard called McKellar, McMillan & Co. She was the first vessel ever to leave
their slipway — Yard Number 1 — and she was launched on the third of October that
year.

The yard was the Lower Woodyard. At its height it employed around five hundred men.
By 1879 — six years after the Georgette left it — the yard was closed and empty.
She was, in that sense, the first and very nearly the last of her line.

## Passage to Australia

Within a year of launching she had left Scotland for Australia, where she would

## Last voyage, 29 November – 1 December 1876

She left Fremantle on 29 November 1876 under sail and steam, three years into
colonial service, carrying fifty passengers and a cargo of jarrah south along the
coast, bound for Adelaide.

By the evening of 30 November, rounding Cape Naturaliste, the ship had begun to
leak. The Chief Engineer reported extra water in the bilge. Captain John Godfrey
was informed. He ordered the pumps checked and did not go below himself — a
decision the [Court of Inquiry](/history/court-of-inquiry-1876) would return to.

Shortly after midnight, midway between Cape Naturaliste and Cape Hamelin, the pumps
failed outright. By 4am on 1 December, passengers and crew were bailing by bucket,
and losing. Near dawn, the rising water reached the boilers and put out the fires.
With a flooding hull and dead engines, Godfrey turned her for the beach. It was the
only option that offered any chance of survival.

She grounded in Calgardup Bay in the early morning of 1 December 1876.

The evacuation, the capsized lifeboat and the rescue are covered in full in

## The wreck today

The Georgette lies a few metres beneath the surface of Calgardup Bay, just off
[Redgate Beach](/history/calgardup-bay). On a calm day — low swell, clear water,
low tide, the sand shifted off her — the shadow of the hull is visible from the
shore and clearly visible from the air. Most days it is not.

### The propellers

When souvenir-hunting divers attempted to salvage the propellers in 1964, one prop
broke in the attempt, a blade snapping off. It was not recovered. The last time it
was seen it had been reclaimed by the tides back into the deep. Attempts to dig it
up in the 1990s failed. It is still there, under the sand at Redgate Beach.

Exactly where it is, and what became of the other propeller, are now part of the
mystery of the SS Georgette.
